Thermal Engineering Analysis

Thermodynamic Mechanics of Closed-Loop Cooling

Cooling systems historically represented up to 40% of total data center electrical demand. As rack densities exceed 50 to 100 kW, traditional air cooling and evaporative towers reach physical limits.

The Water Consumption Baseline

Facility efficiency is quantified using standardized thermodynamic and resource metrics:

PUE =
Total Facility Energy Consumption (kWh) IT Equipment Energy Consumption (kWh)
WUE =
Annual Water Consumption (Liters) IT Equipment Energy Consumption (kWh)
Standardized by ASHRAE Technical Committee 9.9 & The Green Grid

Traditional data centers rely on open-loop evaporative cooling towers. Evaporation dissipates heat via the latent heat of vaporization, consuming between 1.5 and 3.0 Liters per kWh, yielding an industry-average WUE of 1.8 L/kWh. Under peak summer thermal conditions, a large hyperscale facility running open-loop towers consumes between 1 and 5 million gallons of potable water daily—an extraction rate comparable to a city of 30,000 to 50,000 residents.

Approximately 70% to 80% of this water is lost to the atmosphere through evaporation, while the remaining 20% to 30% is discharged into municipal sewers as mineral-dense blowdown wastewater. Furthermore, off-site thermoelectric and hydroelectric generation consumes an additional 7.6 Liters/kWh across the utility supply chain.

Closed-Loop Direct-to-Chip (DLC) Mechanics

To eliminate on-site water consumption, modern architectures deploy closed-loop Direct-to-Chip liquid cooling paired with exterior closed-circuit dry coolers. In this system:

  1. Primary Loop: A sealed primary loop circulates treated dielectric fluid or water through micro-channel cold plates mounted directly against GPUs and CPUs. Heat transfers conductively from silicon into the fluid.
  2. Technology Cooling System (TCS): The hot primary fluid passes through a Technology Cooling System heat exchanger, transferring thermal energy to an isolated secondary loop.
  3. External Dry Coolers: The secondary loop pumps the rejected heat to rooftop or exterior dry coolers equipped with high-efficiency variable-speed fan banks, shedding heat directly into ambient air without any fluid contact with the atmosphere.

Once charged with fluid during commissioning, the closed system operates with zero evaporative water consumption, achieving a verified operational WUE of 0.0 L/kWh and eliminating potable water reliance.
