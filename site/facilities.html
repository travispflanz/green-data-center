100% Solar Baseload

Moro Hub — Dubai, United Arab Emirates

DEWA / Moro Hub · Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum Solar Park

Certified by Guinness World Records as the world's largest solar-powered data center, Moro Hub sits directly inside the Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum Solar Park. It launched with a 6.3 MW phase scaling toward 100 MW of compute capacity, avoiding evaporative water cooling entirely in a severe desert environment.[1]

Geothermal & Hydro Baseload

Verne Global — Keflavík, Iceland

Verne Global · dual-source Icelandic grid

Powered entirely by Iceland's geothermal and hydroelectric transmission grid, Verne Global uses the subarctic maritime climate for 100% ambient free-air economization year-round — no mechanical refrigerants, no evaporative towers.[2]

100% Hydroelectric

Lefdal Mine Datacenter — Måløy, Norway

LMD Operations · former underground olivine mine

Built inside a former underground olivine mine, Lefdal draws 100% renewable power from Norway's regional grid and uses cold fjord seawater heat exchangers — a natural closed-loop thermal sink with zero freshwater evaporation.[3]

SINES DC — Sines, Portugal

Start Campus · Europe's largest planned gigascale renewable campus

Scaling toward 1.2 GW of IT load, SINES is powered by direct PPAs with Portuguese wind and solar assets and cooled by a zero-water ocean-water loop — a coastal siting that pairs renewable generation with a natural heat sink.[4]

24/7 Carbon-Free

St. Ghislain — Belgium

Alphabet (Google) · on-site solar + offsite wind

Google's St. Ghislain campus pairs an on-site utility-scale photovoltaic park behind the meter with regional wind PPAs to meet 24/7 carbon-free energy goals. Its cooling uses industrial canal wastewater evaporation — a non-potable source that keeps WUE at 0.30–0.70 L/kWh.[5]

Closed-loop / zero-water implementations

Zero Potable Evaporation

Microsoft Next-Gen AI Facilities — Phoenix & Wisconsin

Microsoft · Direct-to-Chip + exterior dry coolers

Factory-sealed DLC liquid cooling loops mount cold plates directly on GPU/CPU silicon. Secondary closed loops pipe hot coolant to exterior fin-fan dry coolers — 172 20-foot variable-speed fans per building — with no evaporative towers. The system runs at WUE 0.0 L/kWh and saves over 125 million liters of potable water per facility annually.[7]

100% Liquid-Cooled

NVIDIA DSX AI Factory Architecture

NVIDIA · sealed propylene-glycol loop

Engineered for rack densities above 100 kW, the DSX architecture circulates a sealed mixture of 75% propylene glycol and 25% treated water at return temperatures up to 45°C. Outdoor dry coolers shed 100% of the heat to ambient air — no chillers, no cooling-tower evaporation, zero on-site water consumption.[8]

Sealed Dry-Cooling Coils

Meta Greenfield Hyperscale Campuses

Meta · closed-circuit liquid-to-air coils

Meta has transitioned away from direct evaporative cooling toward sealed closed-circuit liquid-to-air dry cooling coils, delivering hyperscale WUE near 0.20 L/kWh and dropping to 0.0 L/kWh during cooler operating hours.[9]
